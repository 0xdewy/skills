# What Beauty Means, By Task

Read this before working. These are orientations, not rules. They say what
"of course, how else would you do it" looks like in each medium, so the
Student can write the Form sentence and Socrates can test the work against it.

Beauty here is not decoration added after correctness. It is what correct,
necessary, honest work looks like from the outside. So when something reads as
ugly, do not polish it; ask what is wrong underneath. Three sources cover
nearly every case:

- **A lie.** The work claims more than it knows, or presents itself as
  something it is not. Ugliness is the seam where the claim and the fact meet.
- **An unnecessary thing.** An element that serves the author's anxiety, not
  the Form. Remove it and the work gets clearer, not poorer.
- **A wrong proportion.** Attention spent where importance is not. The
  over-elaborated part and the starved part are the same defect.

"Ugly" is therefore never the finding. The finding is the lie, the excess, or
the disproportion, cited where it lives.

## Code

The beautiful program is one a skilled reader meets and thinks: *of course*.

- Names are definitions, not labels. `processData` could be anything;
  `extractInvoiceTotals` cannot be mistaken.
- Each abstraction earns its existence. A function called once is usually a
  distraction; one called from three places names a real pattern.
- The shape of the code mirrors the shape of the problem. Impedance mismatch
  between the two is ugliness.
- Error paths are as considered as the happy path. What the code does on
  failure is what it believes about the world.
- It can be read without being run.

*The Form of code:* a reader who knows the domain but has never seen this
code understands what it does and why within five minutes.

## Writing

The beautiful sentence does one thing completely.

- Specific is beautiful; general is ugly. "Many people" versus "three
  thousand engineers." Specificity is respect for truth.
- Headings are the argument made visible. Reading only the headings should
  reveal the shape of the whole.
- Every word earns its place. Read each sentence aloud and remove what can go.
- It stops when the necessary thing has been said, not when the author runs
  out of things to say.

*The Form of writing:* a reader who disagrees with the conclusion still
recognizes the argument as clear and honestly made.

## Plans and Arguments

- Each step is necessary; a skippable step is not in the plan.
- Dependencies and sequencing are visible.
- Assumptions are named, not buried.
- The reasoning is auditable: why each decision, not only what.
- It has a posture toward failure, not only success.

*The Form of a plan:* a capable stranger could execute it, and if it failed
they would know why.

## Any Other Task

1. What is this for? Does every element serve that?
2. What could be removed? Remove it. Ask again.
3. Does the most important thing get the most attention?
4. Does the work present itself as exactly what it is?
5. Would a careful reader meet it and think *of course*? If they would be
   surprised, the surprise must be intended.

*The Form of anything:* this work could not be otherwise without being
diminished.

Hard acceptance criteria and their verification always come first. No beauty
question is asked while a check fails.
